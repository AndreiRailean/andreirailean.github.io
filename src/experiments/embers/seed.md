## The seed — 2026-09-07

> new experiment: embers
>
> again we're working with dark background. this time we're playing with fire.
> the tiny, irregular shaped embers float up from the bottom and are propelled
> upward by the hot air from below. some go up because a piece of hot wood coal
> splinters and shoots them up in the air. the flow with the strange
> irregularity of a light piece riding hot air in turbulent vortices caused by
> hot and cold air mixing. this gives tham that floaty irregular direction that
> always changes. they accellerate up, then want to fall and get caught in the
> next turbulent flow.
>
> we're looking at it from the side. we can't see the fire, only the embers
> that emerge from it. some disappear from view at the top. others float
> through the side. some zig-zag their way back to the bottom.
>
> let's see what it takes to represent this scene as an experiment. we can
> start with natural fire colours, but later we'll want to let different
> colours ride the flame. we would also want to see how this flow can be
> applied to other pieces of light that may not resemble embers as much.
>
> work independently, as usual. see how far you can get before you get my
> input. i'll have a look at your prototype when built and will provide my
> feedback so we can drive the solution to a pleassant organic-looking gallery
> piece.
>
> there could be gusts of wind to direct the embers and bursts of activity in
> the fire that expel plumes of embers up. i'm hoping for a gorgeous and
> mesmerising simulation that rivals the others we have built.

## 2026-09-12 — heat against hue, and the depth of a fire gradient

> i'm fiddling with controls. there's a "heat" control that claims to set the
> colour of embers but it doesn't seem to do anything. "hue" control is the
> only thing that changes their colour. so either one is unnecessary or I'm
> using them incorrectly. heat slider seems to control the intensity of embers
> and hue controls the colour but hue control seems to make it "flat". embers
> usually appear not as a light to dark gradient but as a transition between
> colours of heat, i.e. yellow-to-white or red-yellow-white. i don't think
> we're going for that kind of realism here but feels that a little bit more
> depth of "fire gradient" is possible.
