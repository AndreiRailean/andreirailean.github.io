A single line grows up from the bottom of the frame. Every so often it decides:
carry on, split into two at opposite angles, turn, stop, or carry on while
throwing off a run of offshoots at one angle, which come out parallel. Whatever
happens on the right happens reflected on the left, so the drawing is always
symmetric about its centre line. Every line is a whole number of turns off
vertical, so lines from opposite ends of the drawing come out parallel too.
When it has finished growing it holds, then undraws in the order it was drawn,
and the next drawing begins.

When a line meets another it decides that too: stop there, cross, or rebound
off it. Circles can land on its corners or along its stretches, drawing
themselves round from their lowest point as the lines grow, and one big circle
can sit on the centre line for the lines to meet.

The symmetry can fold further: top and bottom as well as left and right, so
lines grow in from both edges and meet in the middle, or three, four or six
copies turned round the centre like a kaleidoscope.

Some decisions can come in threes, a beat apart, so a single offshoot becomes
three parallel lines. A tendency to rise makes upright lines run longer and
turns lean back towards upright, for something like a skyline.
