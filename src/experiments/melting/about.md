The wax lies in a pool along the bottom of the lamp. Now and then a lump swells
out of it and rises, the bigger the faster, drawing out behind itself as it
goes; it may split on the way up, the part lagging and sidling off. At the top
the lumps rest and drift together into one, then come apart again and sink back
into the pool. None of the wax is ever made or lost: what is not out is in the
pool, and whatever splits off takes its share with it.

Among the lumps, a few blobs hold their places. They stand on the corners of a
regular solid that is never drawn, flattened straight onto the screen so its
depth is lost: it turns, and they drift, swell a little and run together where
its corners pass over each other. That turning keeps time. A quarter turn takes
as long as it says, the same on every screen, and nothing in it is simulated:
what is drawn at any moment is worked out from that moment alone. Its corners
bud satellites that roam and are swallowed by a neighbour, and every few
seconds a beat arrives, drawn from a stream of values the piece does not
interpret, and tips the solid's axis.

In one scene wax also hangs from the top, as deep as the pool, and the lumps
rise from one layer into the other and back. Turn that screen on its side and
the two layers face each other like paddles: lava pong.
