# seed — melting

What Andrei asked for, in his words. Verbatim. This file faces inward and
records him; `about.md` faces outward and describes the work.

**The piece was called `islands` until 2026-10-24**, when he renamed it below.
The quotes and the address recorded under the old name are left as they were.

**This seed is excerpted, at his instruction.** The piece came out of a longer
conversation about an animation that could also serve as a verifiable timepiece.
He asked that the whole conversation go into the workspace rather than here,
because the forensic side is noise for an experiment —
`~/projects/workspace/timepiece/`. What follows is only the part about the
piece: time-based generation, the hidden shape and its islands. Whole
sentences are quoted unaltered; the sentences between them were left out.

## 2026-10-24 — the seed

> For the unsuspecting observer, it would just show as a strange and fluid
> animation.

> Perhaps we can think of a particular geometric shape of characteristic
> features that mutates between representations, like a 3d cube rotating and
> it's corners are the landmarks that everything evolves out of. Speed of
> rotation could align with the passage of time. Angle of rotation would
> represent the "breed" of the timeline (like a seed that evolves with each
> heartbeat). The cube itself would not be visible, or not be a prominently
> presented as a cube - a set of floating interconnected islands with organic
> features growing out of them.

> Perhaps it doesn't need to maintain time continuity at every instant (every
> frame) but it could sync up to a "time grid" at regular, and very short,
> intervals that make it hard to fake.

## 2026-10-24 — what the experiment is for

> Call the experiment "islands"

> I'd be keen to see if shapes other than a cube make sense and whether
> rotation is what we find most useful. It will be sufficient for an
> experiment, and we may leave it at that. Or we may keep building to have
> better ballance of detectability and visual appeal. I wouldn't want to tax
> the experiments section with maintaining a strict timecode or forensic

> nice if we could feed a stream into experiments to give them more randomness
> and this one opens that door. An experiment itself doesn't necessarily need

> particular kind of variability.

## 2026-10-24 — stretch, and solids that come apart

> going with the lava lamp theme... on some beats, the tumble is rapid.
> perhaps speed of the turn/tumble could influence the blob behaviour.
> currently, it seems, that only the proximity influences the mergers and
> extrusions. as in a true lava lamp, it would be good to see the blobs stretch
> as they move and the stretch would be the likely source of new sattelite
> births. though i also like sattelites' independent motion. when having
> multiple "other solids" on screen, they tend to keep their space and break
> the lava lamp illusion. the main solid moves about in the middle of the
> screen so it somewhat masks its regularity. other solids stay in their
> corners and give up the "secret" much more. There's also a lot of empty
> space. Perhaps "other solids" could move across the screen and be treated
> more like sattelites when they interact with other blobs. Unless we expect
> other solids to also carry detectable time information, we can let them lose
> their "functional integrity" temporarily or as time goes on. if that
> happened, new sattelites would need to be born. that's an interesting idea on
> it's own and perhaps opens a question of lossiness and whether the main
> solid can still perform its function if some of it's blobs vanish and
> re-appeaaar.

## Questions the session could not answer

_The session's, not his._

- **Which name.** He offered three. `melting` was taken because it came first,
  and because a clock that melts is Dalí's; `blobs` and `lava` are a rename
  away (`lava` is also a preset's name).
- **Which shape.** Built as a choice of five regular solids (tetrahedron, cube,
  octahedron, icosahedron, dodecahedron) so it is answered by switching, not
  by asking.
- **Whether rotation is the motion.** `spin` (one axis that drifts at each
  beat) and `tumble` (two axes at once) are both there; neither is assumed.
- **How big a lump may get.** He finds some big enough to hide the shape.
  Nothing caps a lump: its size follows `wax` over `lumps`, and lumps that
  gather at the top run into one bigger still. A cap, or more and smaller
  lumps, are the obvious dials.
- **How the timekeeper should come and go.** `keeper` is the chance it is
  there in each cycle of `cycle` quarter turns; it leaves by gathering into
  one blob that sinks into the pool, and returns the same way. With `change`
  it may also come back as another solid, gathering into one blob in the
  middle and bursting out as the new one. The ways it leaves and changes are
  first guesses.
- **Whether the pool conserves.** `reservoir` runs from the conserved pool
  (0) to the bottomless one he described (1); `tempus` uses 1, every other
  preset 0.
- **Whether colour belongs.** `ora` paints every blob in one of a few hues
  the stream picks, flat, each blob turning all at once. Patterning is not
  built.
- **Whether the clock's blobs and the wax should trade matter.** Today they
  pass through each other and merge where they touch, but a lump never takes
  wax from a corner or leaves any with one. Satellites still bud only from
  the corners.
- **What the stream feeds.** For now the beats come from a local stream —
  a hash of the seed and the beat's number. It tips the axis, times the
  satellites and decides each lump's cycle. The piece does not know what the
  stream is, so a real source (a randomness beacon, audio) can replace it
  without the piece changing. Which variability a stream should drive is open.
